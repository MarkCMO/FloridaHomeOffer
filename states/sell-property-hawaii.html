Hawaii Real Estate Laws Sellers Should Know

Hawaii is an escrow state where escrow companies handle closings. Sellers must provide a Seller's Real Property Disclosure Statement. Conveyance tax ranges from $0.15 to $1.25 per $100 depending on sale price. Hawaii has a unique HARPTA withholding tax of 7.25% on out-of-state sellers.

Understanding these legal differences is important when selling property in Hawaii. A cash sale simplifies the process because there are no lender requirements, no appraisal contingencies, and the closing timeline is driven by the title search and document preparation - not bank approvals.

Tax Considerations When Selling Hawaii Property for Cash

Whether you sell to OneCashOffer or list traditionally, the IRS tax treatment of your sale is the same. If your Hawaii property has been your primary residence for at least two of the last five years, you can exclude up to $250,000 of capital gains ($500,000 if married filing jointly) under IRS Section 121.

For inherited Hawaii property, you receive a stepped-up cost basis equal to the property's fair market value at the date of death - which often eliminates capital gains tax entirely. Investment properties may qualify for a 1031 exchange to defer capital gains by reinvesting proceeds into another property within 180 days.

We are not tax professionals. For specific Hawaii property-tax questions, consult a CPA or visit the IRS Topic 701 page on selling your home.
